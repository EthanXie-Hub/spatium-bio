# L1.6-2026-05-30 — Pipeline + first family sample

- **What I was trying to do**: stop hand-filling result JSONs, add a
  CLI, and run a small multi-family sample before scaling the model.
- **Time on this**: two evenings at a Starbucks, looking things up
  whenever I hit something I didn't know.

---

## What happened

Built the experiment pipeline as reusable code instead of a one-off
script:

- `spatium_bio.compare` — load a targets manifest, fetch + embed each
  protein, build the cosine matrix, summarise within- vs across-family
  similarity, and record provenance (timestamp, git commit, library
  versions, device).
- `spatium-bio embed-compare --targets … --out …` CLI (argparse).
- Two manifests: a 3-sequence sanity set and a 12-sequence L2-prep set
  spanning globin / ubiquitin-like / lysozyme / an unrelated out-group.

The 3-sequence run reproduced L1 exactly (0.9781 / 0.6926 / 0.7078), so
the refactor didn't change the numbers — good.

The 12-sequence run:

```
within-family  mean 0.8502  (n=13 pairs)
across-family  mean 0.7263  (n=53 pairs)
separation          +0.1240
```

## What surprised me

Two of my "families" came out at exactly 1.0000 within-family:

```
globin          0.9756  (n=3)
lysozyme_c      1.0000  (n=3)
ubiquitin_like  1.0000  (n=1)
other           0.6877  (n=6)
```

My first thought was that I'd written something wrong and the
calculation was broken. Then I looked at the RCSB titles the script
prints next to each entry: 1HEL, 6LYZ and 1LYZ are all the **same
protein** — hen egg-white lysozyme — just different crystal structures.
Same sequence in, identical embedding out, cosine 1.0. Same story for
the two ubiquitins (1UBQ / 1UBI). The 1.0 wasn't a bug; my
"within-family similarity" was, for two of four families, really just
"the same sequence compared to itself."

The only family in the set with genuinely different sequences sharing a
fold is globin (Hb α, Hb β, myoglobin), at 0.976.

## What this does not prove

- The +0.124 separation is real but partly inflated by the duplicate
  sequences. The honest headline is narrower: *for genuinely distinct
  globins, cosine stays ~0.98, while unrelated proteins sit lower.*
- Still n = tiny, still no baseline (random vectors, k-mer, BLOSUM),
  still no structural metric (TM-score).
- `other` is a grab-bag, not a family, so its 0.69 "within" mean is
  meaningless — it's the average over four unrelated proteins.

## Takeaway

I think someone teaching themselves has to go one step at a time —
learn to walk before trying to run, or you fall a lot. And this is
heading toward real science, so I'd rather be honest and rigorous than
fast. Doing the small, careful run first is the only reason I caught
the redundant-sequence problem at all.

If I'd jumped straight to the 650M model on 30 proteins, yes — I could
have buried this inside a better-looking score. But I'd have skipped
the 0-to-1 part, which is the foundation of teaching yourself. The
numbers would look good; what I'd actually lose is the experience. I'd
rather go one step at a time, even if it's slower — the process matters
more to me, and it's what will make me get less wrong, or nothing
wrong, later.

The cross-check that prints each target's RCSB title next to my family
label also worked — every label matched, so at least I didn't mislabel
anything.
